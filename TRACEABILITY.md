## 3. Detailed Traceability Walkthrough

### 3.1 Trace 1: The Jackson-Zave Boundary (Lecture 1)
- **Concept:** In requirements engineering (Jackson-Zave framework, Lecture 1 pp. 62–95), the system is divided into Environment Phenomena ($E$), Machine Phenomena ($M$), and Shared Phenomena ($S$). Requirements ($REQ$) live in the environment; specifications ($SPEC$) live on the interface; assumptions ($ASM$) describe environment behavior. The core theorem states:
  $$SPEC \wedge ASM \Rightarrow REQ$$
- **Application in SAMT:**
  - $E$: Ground corrosion, underground pipe wall shear, residential tap openings, rooftop tank float valve dynamics.
  - $S$: Ultrasonic flow meter pulses, piezoresistive pressure transducer voltages, field repair crew status reports.
  - $M$: Trapezoidal volume integration, 02:00–04:00 night-window filtering, priority-weighted water allocation.
  - $ASM$: Network boundary valves are tight; unmetered municipal water consumption between 02:00 and 04:00 is less than 5% of diurnal peak; tank refills follow a known float-valve chattering profile.
- **Trace in Code:** [`waterBalance.ts`](file:///d:/github_repos/water/prototype/src/domain/balance/waterBalance.ts) lines 12–30 explicitly documents and outputs these assumptions as part of the `WaterBalanceResult`.

### 3.2 Trace 2: Information Hiding & Interchangeable Adapters (Lecture 4)
- **Concept:** David Parnas defined a module as "a responsibility assignment, not a subprogram," with each module hiding a design decision or secret likely to change (Lecture 4 pp. 20–53). The Dependency Inversion Principle (DIP, pp. 65–106) mandates that high-level policy does not depend on low-level details.
- **Application in SAMT:**
  - *Secret 1: Time source.* Encapsulated by [`Clock`](file:///d:/github_repos/water/prototype/src/domain/ports/Clock.ts).
  - *Secret 2: Incident persistence.* Encapsulated by [`IIncidentRepository`](file:///d:/github_repos/water/prototype/src/domain/ports/IncidentPort.ts).
  - Two interchangeable adapters implement `IIncidentRepository`:
    1. [`InMemoryIncidentRepository`](file:///d:/github_repos/water/prototype/src/domain/adapters/InMemoryIncidentRepository.ts): mutable Map with optimistic concurrency control (`version` check).
    2. [`ValidatedJsonFixtureIncidentRepository`](file:///d:/github_repos/water/prototype/src/domain/adapters/ValidatedJsonFixtureIncidentRepository.ts): pre-populated from validated JSON fixtures with copy-on-write semantics.
- **Trace in Tests:** [`portConformance.test.ts`](file:///d:/github_repos/water/prototype/src/tests/portConformance.test.ts) runs a parameterised suite of 8 identical contract tests against both repositories.

### 3.3 Trace 3: Controllability Seam for Nocturnal Hydraulics (Lecture 5)
- **Concept:** Controllability is the ability to place a software component into any specified internal state to execute a test; observability is the ability to inspect the resulting state (Robert Binder, Lecture 5 pp. 6–25).
- **Application in SAMT:**
  - Minimum Night Flow (MNF) analysis must run strictly between 02:00 and 04:00 Asia/Baku time (AZT, UTC+4). Without a seam, automated tests would fail or require sleeping for hours until actual midnight.
  - [`FixedClock`](file:///d:/github_repos/water/prototype/src/domain/ports/Clock.ts) allows the test suite to inject `2026-10-20T03:00:00+04:00` deterministically.
- **Trace in Tests:** [`nightFlowEvaluation.test.ts`](file:///d:/github_repos/water/prototype/src/tests/nightFlowEvaluation.test.ts) asserts night window detection and nocturnal flow spike evaluation in 1 millisecond.
